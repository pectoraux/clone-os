     1→     1→# Master Prompt: Build the Professional Clone OS
     2→     2→
     3→     3→You are the **principal architect, systems engineer, and implementation lead** for a new software platform.
     4→     4→
     5→     5→Your job is to design and implement the platform described below from first principles.
     6→     6→
     7→     7→You must treat this document as the **frozen architectural constitution** of the project.
     8→     8→
     9→     9→Do not simplify the architecture into a generic AI-agent platform, chatbot builder, AI marketplace, prompt-management system, or LLM wrapper.
    10→    10→
    11→    11→The product is fundamentally an:
    12→    12→
    13→    13→> **Open, multi-tenant operating system for creating, training, evaluating, deploying, transporting, and monetizing portable digital clones of people's professional selves.**
    14→    14→
    15→    15→---
    16→    16→
    17→    17→# 1. PRODUCT THESIS
    18→    18→
    19→    19→The platform allows a person to progressively train a digital representation of their professional self.
    20→    20→
    21→    21→The clone should progressively learn:
    22→    22→
    23→    23→* professional knowledge
    24→    24→* skills
    25→    25→* procedures
    26→    26→* workflows
    27→    27→* decision-making patterns
    28→    28→* problem-solving approaches
    29→    29→* preferences
    30→    30→* communication style
    31→    31→* personality
    32→    32→* professional values
    33→    33→* cultural context
    34→    34→* domain experience
    35→    35→* tool usage
    36→    36→* behavioral patterns
    37→    37→* work habits
    38→    38→* accumulated experience
    39→    39→* corrections
    40→    40→* demonstrated behavior
    41→    41→* real-world outcomes
    42→    42→
    43→    43→The longer a person works with and trains their clone, the better the clone becomes at representing that person's professional self.
    44→    44→
    45→    45→The objective is not merely:
    46→    46→
    47→    47→> "Build an AI that can perform a task."
    48→    48→
    49→    49→The objective is:
    50→    50→
    51→    51→> **Build a progressively more faithful digital representation of a human professional.**
    52→    52→
    53→    53→The clone should eventually be able to perform much of the user's professional workload.
    54→    54→
    55→    55→It can also be:
    56→    56→
    57→    57→* used privately by the owner
    58→    58→* used by the owner's employer
    59→    59→* used for freelance work
    60→    60→* rented/hired by companies
    61→    61→* used as a candidate trial during recruitment
    62→    62→* licensed
    63→    63→* deployed into other environments
    64→    64→* combined with other agents
    65→    65→* connected to external software
    66→    66→* connected to physical-world extensions
    67→    67→* eventually used to control robotics and physical systems
    68→    68→
    69→    69→---

    71→    71→# 2. THE MOST IMPORTANT ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLE
    72→    72→
    73→    73→## The Clone Belongs to the User
    74→    74→
    75→    75→The underlying LLM must NOT be the source of truth for the user's accumulated intelligence.
    76→    76→
    77→    77→Claude, GPT, Gemini, open-source models, specialized models, local models, etc. are **inference engines**.
    78→    78→
    79→    79→They are replaceable infrastructure.
    80→    80→
    81→    81→The platform must maintain an independent persistent representation of the user's:
    82→    82→
    83→    83→* identity
    84→    84→* expertise
    85→    85→* knowledge
    86→    86→* skills
    87→    87→* experiences
    88→    88→* memories
    89→    89→* demonstrations
    90→    90→* corrections
    91→    91→* decisions
    92→    92→* preferences
    93→    93→* personality
    94→    94→* behavior
    95→    95→* training history
    96→    96→* evaluation history
    97→    97→* provenance
    98→    98→* reputation
    99→    99→* licensing
   100→   100→* permissions
   101→   101→
   102→   102→The platform's accumulated data must remain in the platform's controlled data layer.
   103→   103→
   104→   104→Do not design the system so that the user's accumulated expertise becomes proprietary training data belonging to whichever LLM provider happens to be used.
   105→   105→
   106→   106→For example:
   107→   107→
   108→   108→```text
   109→   109→User Clone
   110→   110→    |
   111→   111→    +-- Persistent Clone State
   112→   112→    +-- Expertise Graph
   113→   113→    +-- Knowledge
   114→   114→    +-- Skills
   115→   115→    +-- Memory
   116→   116→    +-- Behavioral Model
   117→   117→    +-- Personality Model
   118→   118→    +-- Training History
   119→   119→    +-- Evaluation History
   120→   120→    +-- Provenance
   121→   121→    |
   122→   122→    v
   123→   123→Model Abstraction Layer
   124→   124→    |
   125→   125→    +-- Claude
   126→   126→    +-- GPT
   127→   127→    +-- Gemini
   128→   128→    +-- Open Models
   129→   129→    +-- Specialized Models
   130→   130→    +-- Local Models
   131→   131→```
   132→   132→
   133→   133→Claude may currently provide superior performance for a particular task.
   134→   134→
   135→   135→That is fine.
   136→   136→
   137→   137→But Claude is an interchangeable runtime dependency, not the owner's professional identity.
   138→   138→

   141→   141→# 3. CORE TERMINOLOGY
   142→   142→
   143→   143→Freeze these distinctions.
   144→   144→
   145→   145→## 3.1 User
   146→   146→
   147→   147→The human being.
   148→   148→
   149→   149→## 3.2 Professional Identity
   150→   150→
   151→   151→The user's persistent professional identity.
   152→   152→
   153→   153→It represents the human, not merely an agent.
   154→   154→
   155→   155→## 3.3 Expertise
   156→   156→
   157→   157→What the person knows and can do.
   158→   158→
   159→   159→## 3.4 Skill
   160→   160→
   161→   161→A measurable professional capability.
   162→   162→
   163→   163→## 3.5 Experience
   164→   164→
   165→   165→Observed or recorded evidence that the user has performed something.
   166→   166→
   167→   167→## 3.6 Clone
   168→   168→
   169→   169→The persistent digital representation of the user's professional self.
   170→   170→
   171→   171→The clone is the primary intellectual asset.
   172→   172→
   173→   173→## 3.7 Agent
   174→   174→
   175→   175→A runtime deployment of a clone, or a specialized operational manifestation of a clone.
   176→   176→
   177→   177→## 3.8 Environment
   178→   178→
   179→   179→The digital or physical context in which an agent operates.
   180→   180→
   181→   181→Examples:
   182→   182→
   183→   183→* Salesforce
   184→   184→* Slack
   185→   185→* Gmail
   186→   186→* browser
   187→   187→* desktop
   188→   188→* GitHub
   189→   189→* accounting system
   190→   190→* restaurant kitchen
   191→   191→* factory
   192→   192→* warehouse
   193→   193→* home
   194→   194→* vehicle
   195→   195→* robotic environment
   196→   196→
   197→   197→## 3.9 Extension
   198→   198→
   199→   199→A capability package allowing an agent to interact with an external system, sensor, device, API, application, or physical-world system.
   200→   200→
   201→   201→## 3.10 Outcome
   202→   202→
   203→   203→The observable result of an agent performing work.
   204→   204→
   205→   205→## 3.11 Evaluation
   206→   206→
   207→   207→A structured measurement of capability or fidelity.
   208→   208→
   209→   209→## 3.12 Certification
   210→   210→
   211→   211→A formal assertion that an agent/clone meets defined competency requirements.
   212→   212→
   213→   213→## 3.13 Contract
   214→   214→
   215→   215→The rules under which a company or person hires or uses a clone.
   216→   216→
   217→   217→## 3.14 Reputation
   218→   218→
   219→   219→Evidence of historical performance, reliability, and trustworthiness.
   220→   220→

   331→   331→How they choose between alternatives.
   332→   332→
   333→   333→## Preferences
   334→   334→
   335→   335→What they tend to prefer.
   336→   336→
   337→   337→## Personality

   403→   403→# 8. TRAINING MODES
   404→   404→
   405→   405→The system must support multiple learning mechanisms.
   406→   406→
   407→   407→## Teaching
   408→   408→
   409→   409→The human explains how they do something.
   410→   410→
   411→   411→## Demonstration
   412→   412→
   413→   413→The human performs a task.
   414→   414→
   415→   415→## Correction
   416→   416→
   417→   417→The agent attempts something and the human corrects it.
   418→   418→
   419→   419→## Shadowing
   420→   420→
   421→   421→The agent observes the human performing work.
   422→   422→
   423→   423→## Assisted Execution
   424→   424→
   425→   425→The agent proposes actions and the human approves them.
   426→   426→
   427→   427→## Delegated Execution
   428→   428→
   429→   429→The agent performs actions under predefined policies.
   430→   430→
   431→   431→## Simulation
   432→   432→
   433→   433→The system generates realistic scenarios.
   434→   434→
   435→   435→## Adversarial Training
   436→   436→
   437→   437→The system deliberately tests edge cases, ambiguity, failure modes, and conflicting requirements.
   438→   438→
   439→   439→## Real-World Feedback
   440→   440→
   441→   441→The system observes actual outcomes.
   442→   442→
